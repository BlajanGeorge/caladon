# Caladon — Buildings proposal (research + numbers)

**Status: accepted (design only, nothing implemented).** Companion to the *Buildings* and *Army* sections of
ARCHITECTURE.md, which record the rules; this file holds the numbers and is the source of the config tables.

All numbers below are generated by `docs/buildings_tables.py` from the formulas in §3, not typed by hand.
Sources for the reference games are in §8.

## 1. What the reference games do (summary of the research)

**Tribal Wars** (InnoGames): 3 land resources (wood/clay/iron), 15–19 buildings, everything driven by
`base × factor^(level−1)` with one factor per resource per building (verified against the game's own
`buildings.xml`). Points per building level are `P1 × 1.2^(level−1)` exactly. Mines produce
`30 × 1.163118^(level−1)` per hour (2 400/h at level 30); farm holds `240 × 100^((level−1)/29)` (24 000 at
30); warehouse `1 000 × 1.2294934^(level−1)` (400 000 at 30). Build time `base × 1.18 × 1.2^(L−1−14/(L−1)) ×
1.05^(−HQ)`. Wall +3.7 % defence per level compounding; Barracks/Stable/Workshop recruit at `2/3 × 1.06^(−L)`;
Market merchants `L` up to 10 then `(L−10)² + 10`. New village: HQ 1, Farm 1, Warehouse 1, Hiding place 1,
Rally point 1 = 26 points, 7 of 240 population used.

**Grepolis** (InnoGames): naval game, 3 resources (wood/stone/silver) plus favour and culture, 14 regular
buildings + 8 special (choose 2). Costs and effects are hand-tuned tables rather than exponentials; mines
cap at 275–426/h at level 40, farm at 3 560, warehouse at 28 142; points grow ≈ ×1.08–1.14 per level with
very different bases per building (Temple 216 at level 1, Timber Camp 22). New city: Senate 1, Agora,
Farm 1, Warehouse 1 = 175 points, 114 population. Senate speeds construction by a non-linear table
(44.5 % at level 25).

**Takeaways for Caladon**
- Use the Tribal Wars numbers **1:1**: one exponential per resource per building, points `P1 × 1.2^(L−1)`,
  30/h, 1 000 capacity, 240 population at level 1. A pace proven over twenty years, and every table can be
  lifted verbatim. The earlier gut values (30/min, 2 000, 100, 50 points) are replaced.
- Both games keep the farm and warehouse **free of population cost**; both make resource buildings the
  cheapest and the military buildings the most expensive per level; both gate everything through a
  headquarters/senate that also speeds construction.
- Grepolis's special-building "pick 2 of 8" is a nice late-game choice but needs the naval/culture systems
  it plugs into; skip for now.

## 2. Proposed roster

Nine buildings. The six marked *founded* exist at level 1 in every new city and cost nothing at level 1
(Town Hall included: it gates the others, speeds construction and owns the build queue). The other three are
built by the player.

| code | name | max | founded | job | level-1 value | points L1 (+gain at L2 / L10 / max) | prerequisites |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `FARM` | Farm | 30 | yes | population | 240 (+240) | 5 (+1 / +5 / +165) | — |
| `WOODCUTTER` | Woodcutter | 30 | yes | wood/h | 30/h | 6 (+1 / +5 / +198) | — |
| `STONE_MINE` | Stone Mine | 30 | yes | stone/h | 30/h | 6 (+1 / +5 / +198) | — |
| `IRON_MINE` | Iron Mine | 30 | yes | iron/h | 30/h | 6 (+1 / +5 / +198) | — |
| `DEPOSIT` | Deposit | 30 | yes | capacity | 1,000 | 6 (+1 / +5 / +198) | — |
| `TOWN_HALL` | Town Hall | 30 | yes | build speed, gates | 95% build time, queue 2 | 10 (+2 / +9 / +330) | — |
| `BARRACKS` | Barracks | 25 | no | recruits all troops; level unlocks unit types | 63% recruit time | 16 (+3 / +14 / +212) | Town Hall 3 |
| `ACADEMY` | Academy | 20 | no | studies unit types; level gates what can be studied | 91% study time | 19 (+4 / +16 / +101) | Town Hall 8, Farm 6, Barracks 5 |
| `WALL` | Wall | 20 | no | defence bonus | +4% defence | 8 (+2 / +7 / +43) | Town Hall 5 |
| `VAULT` | Vault | 10 | no | hides resources | 150 per resource | 5 (+1 / +5 / +5) | Town Hall 5, Deposit 5 |

A new city therefore starts with **39 points** (29 without Town Hall), **240 free population**,
**30/h** of each resource, **500** of each in stock and a **1,000** cap.

Notes on the choices:
- **Rally Point** (Tribal Wars) is *not* a building: commanding troops is always available from the
  Barracks/city screen. One less thing to build for no decision value.
- **Vault** = Tribal Wars' Hiding Place, renamed to fit *Deposit*; hides a per-resource amount from
  plunder. Cheap, 10 levels, optional to build. If plunder is never implemented it can be dropped.
- **One recruitment building.** Tribal Wars splits recruitment over Barracks / Stable / Workshop and gates
  unit types through the Smithy. Caladon collapses that: the **Barracks** recruits every unit type, and its
  **level** decides which types are available; the **Academy** is where a unit type is *studied* before it
  can be recruited, and its level decides which types can be studied. See *Unit gating* below.
- **Academy numbers** are Tribal Wars' Smithy (cost, population, points, 20 levels, study-time bonus), since
  it takes over that role. Conquest / nobles are **not** in the Academy; they get their own building later.
- **No Market.** There is no trade between cities in Caladon; resources are earned by production and
  plunder only.
- Skipped on purpose: Smithy, Stable, Workshop (folded into Barracks + Academy), Rally Point, Statue/Paladin
  (hero system), Church/Temple (faith), Watchtower (can come later as an optional 20-level building),
  Harbour/Lighthouse (naval), Cave (silver-specific), Agora (UI, not a building), Grepolis's special buildings.

### Unit gating (Barracks level × Academy study)

A unit type can be recruited in a city when **both** hold: the city's Barracks is at or above the type's
*Barracks level*, and the type has been *studied* in that city's Academy. Studying a type is a one-time
purchase per city (resources, no population) that requires the Academy at or above the type's *Academy
level*; the Academy's level also shortens study time (`1.1^(−L)`). The first unit needs no study so a
Barracks-1 city can recruit at once. The ladder below is **illustrative** — the units themselves are the
next design; only the gating mechanism is proposed here.

### Level requirements

Two kinds of conditions, both checked at order time:

1. **Town Hall step rule (every building except the Town Hall itself).** To reach level `L` the Town Hall
   must be at least `5 × floor((L−1)/5)`: levels 2–5 need nothing, 6–10 need Town Hall 5, 11–15 need 10,
   16–20 need 15, 21–25 need 20, 26–30 need 25. So a city cannot sit at Town Hall 3 with level-30 mines;
   the Town Hall always trails the tallest building by at most five levels.
2. **Cross-building conditions** — to build a player building at all, and again at a few higher levels
   (Grepolis style: the Vault needs a Deposit, the Academy needs a Barracks, the Wall a Stone Mine;
   the Barracks depends on the Town Hall only, one step tighter than the step rule).

| building | to build (level 1) | at higher levels (in addition to the step rule) |
|---|---|---|
| Farm | founded | step rule only |
| Woodcutter | founded | step rule only |
| Stone Mine | founded | step rule only |
| Iron Mine | founded | step rule only |
| Deposit | founded | step rule only |
| Town Hall | founded | none (it is the gate) |
| Barracks | Town Hall 3 | L10: Town Hall 10, L20: Town Hall 20 |
| Academy | Town Hall 8, Farm 6, Barracks 5 | L10: Barracks 10, L15: Barracks 15 |
| Wall | Town Hall 5 | L10: Stone Mine 10, L15: Stone Mine 15 |
| Vault | Town Hall 5, Deposit 5 | L5: Deposit 10 |


## 3. Formulas (the knobs)

```
cost[r](L)      = round(COST_SCALE × base_r × f_r^(L−1))        COST_SCALE = 1; base/f per building in §8
popTotal(L)     = round(POP_SCALE × base_pop × f_pop^(L−1))     POP_SCALE = 1; popCost(L) = popTotal(L) − popTotal(L−1)
points(L)       = round(P1 × 1.2^(L−1))                     cumulative; the city gains points(L) − points(L−1) per upgrade
production(L)   = round(30 × 1.163118^(L−1)) per hour          Woodcutter / Stone Mine / Iron Mine; 2,400/h at 30
capacity(L)     = round(1000 × 1.2294934^(L−1))              Deposit, per resource; 400,000 at 30
farmPop(L)      = floor(240 × 100^((L−1)/29))              Farm; farmGain(L) = farmPop(L) − farmPop(L−1); 24,000 at 30
buildTime(L)    = (base_s / TIME_SCALE) × 1.18 × 1.2^(L−1−14/(L−1)) × 1.05^(−TownHall)   TIME_SCALE = 1; exponent = −13 for L ≤ 2
townHall(L)     = build time × 1.05^(−L)                          (95 % at 1 … 23 % at 30)
queue(L)        = 2 slots; 3 from Town Hall 10; 4 from Town Hall 20   (orders waiting in the build queue, all paid at placement)
recruit(L)      = unit time × 2/3 × 1.06^(−L)                      Barracks (all unit types)
study(L)        = study time × 1.1^(−L)                            Academy (Tribal Wars Smithy fit, community, not official)
wall(L)         = defender strength × 1.037^L                      (+107 % at 20)
vault(L)        = round(150 × (4/3)^(L−1)) hidden per resource     (150 → 1,998; TW uses a table for the last levels: 1 125 / 1 500 / 2 000)
```

Why these knobs:
- **COST_SCALE 1, POP_SCALE 1, TIME_SCALE 1: Tribal Wars 1:1.** They stay as knobs so a faster world
  variant is one constant, not a redesign; but the intended way to run faster worlds is the **world speed**
  multiplier already in the Resources design (it scales production and build times together).
- **Rates per hour**, as in Tribal Wars: 30/h is 0.5/min and per-minute numbers read badly. The Resources
  section now says per hour; the implemented `ratePerMinute` field becomes `ratePerHour` (the settlement
  math is a unit conversion).
- **Points ×1.2 with Tribal Wars P1 values.** A maxed mine is worth 1,187 points, a maxed Farm
  989, a fully built city (every building at max) 9,876. The existing map
  tier thresholds (t1 < 1 000, t2 < 5 000, t3 ≥ 5 000) work as they are; with five tier sprites available,
  proposal: t1 < 1 000, t2 < 3 000, t3 < 6 000, t4 < 9 000, t5 ≥ 9 000.
- **Population.** Farm 240 at level 1, 24,000 at 30; all buildings maxed use 3,002, leaving
  20,998 for troops. Founding buildings cost no population, so a new city has all 240 free
  (Tribal Wars: 233).

## 7. Buildings with their own behaviour

### Deposit

Nothing beyond capacity: `capacity(L)` per resource (table in §4); production above it is lost.

### Vault — what it protects

An attacker who wins can take, per resource, at most `stock − vault(L)`, further limited by the carry
capacity of the surviving attacking units. The Vault does nothing else. The curve is Tribal Wars' Hiding
place (the last three levels use its table values):

| Vault level | hidden per resource | Deposit at the same level | share protected |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 150 | 1,000 | 15 % |
| 2 | 200 | 1,229 | 16 % |
| 3 | 267 | 1,512 | 18 % |
| 4 | 356 | 1,859 | 19 % |
| 5 | 474 | 2,285 | 21 % |
| 6 | 632 | 2,810 | 22 % |
| 7 | 843 | 3,454 | 24 % |
| 8 | 1,125 | 4,247 | 26 % |
| 9 | 1,500 | 5,222 | 29 % |
| 10 | 2,000 | 6,420 | 31 % |

At level 10 it is maxed: a developed city with a level-20 Deposit (50 675) protects only 2 000 per
resource. As in Tribal Wars, the Vault is early-game protection, not a late-game one.

**Recruitment rules**
- A unit type can be recruited when the Barracks is at or above its *Barracks ≥* level **and** the type has
  been studied in this city's Academy (Spearman needs no study).
- Recruit time = `base × 2/3 × 1.06^(−Barracks level)`: 63 % of base at level 1, 37 % at 10, 21 % at 20,
  16 % at 25. Above level 20 the Barracks unlocks nothing new; it only gets faster.
- One recruitment queue per city. An order pays resources and population at placement (as buildings do);
  units complete one at a time; cancelling refunds the unproduced remainder.
- The **Nobleman** is the conquest unit: studied at Academy 20, recruited from Barracks 20. Its stats and
  cost are Tribal Wars' (40 000 / 50 000 / 50 000, 100 population, 5 h base time); the conquest mechanics
  (loyalty, how many nobles a city can hold, Tribal Wars' extra coin cost per noble) are a later design.
  Mounted archer, Paladin and Militia are left out.

### Academy — what can be studied per level

Studying a unit type is a one-time purchase per city (resources, no population). It needs the Academy at
or above the type's level and takes `studyBase × 1.1^(−Academy level)`. Tribal Wars does not publish its
research durations, so **studyBase = 2 × the unit's recruit base time** is a placeholder. Study costs are
Tribal Wars' simple-tech research costs; the Nobleman's study cost is Tribal Wars' Academy building cost (the
price of gaining the ability to make nobles there), as a placeholder.
